OVERVIEW

What is Dissolution of Society?

Dissolution of society is the legal process of permanently closing a registered society by obtaining a 3/5th majority vote of total members under Section 13 of the Societies Registration Act, 1860, settling all liabilities, transferring surplus assets to a similar organisation under Section 14, and obtaining a dissolution certificate from the Registrar of Societies.

A critical rule: surplus property cannot be distributed among members. Section 14 mandates that all surplus assets after settling debts must transfer to another society with similar objectives. This ensures assets accumulated through public contributions are preserved for their intended purpose.

GROUNDS FOR DISSOLUTION

Grounds for Dissolution of Society

01

Purpose Achieved

The society was formed for a specific objective that has been completed. Dissolution avoids unnecessary compliance burdens.

02

Membership Below Minimum

Membership has fallen below the statutory minimum of 7 members under Section 1. Recruitment of new members is not feasible.

03

Financial Insolvency

Resources exhausted, donor support ceased, and liabilities exceed assets. Continued operation is financially unviable.

04

Inactivity for 2+ Years

No meaningful activities for 2 or more consecutive years. Registrar can also initiate suo motu dissolution for 3+ years of inactivity.

05

Internal Disputes

Irreconcilable disputes among members or office bearers prevent effective governance. Court-ordered dissolution is common in such cases.

06

Merger or Restructuring

The society is merging with another organisation or restructuring into a Section 8 Company or Trust.

PROCESS

Step-by-Step Society Dissolution Process

1. Review Society Bye-Laws and Member Registry

Examine MOA, bye-laws and rules for dissolution provisions. Verify the complete member registry to calculate the 3/5th majority threshold under Section 13.

2. Draft Special Resolution and Issue EGM Notice

Draft the dissolution resolution specifying grounds and Section 14 surplus plan. Issue written notice to all members with at least 14 days’ lead time.

3. Conduct EGM and Pass Special Resolution

Conduct the EGM with proper quorum. Obtain consent of at least 3/5th (60%) of total members, not just those present. Record detailed minutes and get the resolution signed.

4. Settle All Liabilities and Prepare Final Accounts

Clear all debts, employee settlements and statutory obligations. Engage a professional to prepare final audited accounts certified up to the dissolution date.

5. Publish Dissolution Notice in Newspaper

Publish a notice in at least 1 local newspaper (2 in Maharashtra) inviting objections within 30 days. Keep the published copy as proof for the Registrar.

6. File Dissolution Application with Registrar

Submit the application with special resolution, final accounts, no-liability affidavit, member list, newspaper proof and asset distribution plan. Fee ₹50–₹500.

7. Transfer Surplus Assets per Section 14

Identify a recipient society with similar objectives. Execute transfer deeds for immovable property and transfer movable assets and bank balances. Members cannot receive surplus.

8. Cancel Tax and Regulatory Registrations

Cancel 12A/80G (Form 10AB), deregister FCRA if applicable, cancel GST (REG-16) and file GSTR-10, and surrender the society’s PAN.

9. Obtain Dissolution Certificate from Registrar

After verification and expiry of the objection period, the Registrar issues the dissolution order, which is published in the Official Gazette. Retain records for at least 8 years.

SURPLUS PROPERTY (SEC 14)

Surplus Property Distribution – Section 14 Rules

01

No Distribution to Members

Surplus property shall not be paid to or distributed among members. This is a non-negotiable rule. Violating it exposes office bearers to personal legal liability.

02

Transfer to Similar Organisation

Surplus must transfer to another society with similar objectives, determined by 3/5th of members at the dissolution meeting or as directed by the Registrar/court.

03

Property Transfer Documentation

For immovable property: registered transfer deeds with stamp duty (typically 5%–7%). For movable assets and bank balances: transfer inventory and demand drafts to the recipient.

04

Specific Property Claims

Members who contributed specific identifiable property (not cash) may have a claim for return of that property under general trust principles—distinct from a claim on surplus.

POST-DISSOLUTION

After Dissolution: Post-Dissolution Compliance

01

Cancel 12A / 80G

File Form 10AB on the Income Tax portal to surrender 12A and 80G registrations within the prescribed timeline.

02

FCRA Deregistration

If FCRA-registered, file Form FC-7 and apply for deregistration with the Ministry of Home Affairs.

03

GST Cancellation & PAN Surrender

Cancel GST via REG-16, file GSTR-10 if required, and surrender the society’s PAN after assessment.

04

NGO Darpan & Records

Update status on NITI Aayog NGO Darpan if registered. Retain all dissolution records for at least 8 years.

COMMON MISTAKES

Common Mistakes to Avoid

01

Counting Only Present Members

The 3/5th majority is calculated on total membership, not members present at the EGM. If the society has 100 members, 60 must consent.

02

Skipping Newspaper Publication

Most states require a published notice with a 30-day objection window. Filing without this proof results in Registrar rejection.

03

Distributing Surplus to Members

Section 14 strictly prohibits this. Any such distribution can be challenged in court and attracts personal liability for office bearers.

04

Incomplete Member Notice

EGM notice must be sent to all members at their registered addresses with at least 14 days’ lead time and delivery proof retained.
