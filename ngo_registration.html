CHOOSE THE RIGHT STRUCTURE

NGO Registration Types

India has no single "NGO Act". Every non-profit is registered under one of three laws, and the right one usually follows your funding plan.

S8
MOST FUNDED

Section 8 Company

A corporate non-profit structure suited to organisations expecting CSR, institutional or government funding, or working across multiple states.

  • Minimum 2 directors & 2 members
  • Memorandum in Form INC-13
  • Licence granted with incorporation
  • Operates in every state
Compare Structures →
TR

Trust

The fastest and simplest route for a founder-led charitable initiative, created by a deed registered before the Sub-Registrar.

  • Settlor plus 2 trustees minimum
  • Trust deed on stamp paper
  • Lightest ongoing compliance
  • Suitable for individual donors
Compare Structures →
SO

Society

A membership-based structure for organisations that should be run by an elected governing council answerable to a general body.

  • 7 or more subscribers
  • Memorandum & rules drafted
  • Registered with the state Registrar
  • Suitable for membership bodies

WHY FORMALLY REGISTER?

Turn Your Mission Into a Recognised Legal Entity.

Registration gives your organisation legal personality of its own, separate from its founders, and is the foundation for tax exemption, grants and donor confidence.

01

Legal Recognition

A registered NGO can hold property, a bank account and enter contracts in its own name, subject to applicable law.

02

Tax Benefit Eligibility

Registration is a precondition for applying for income-tax exemption and donor deduction approvals.

03

Donor & Grant Credibility

A formal structure can increase confidence among individual donors, CSR teams and grant-making bodies.

04

Access to Funding Routes

Registered NGOs can pursue CSR funding, government grants and, where eligible, foreign contributions.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NGO registration? +

NGO registration is the legal process of giving a charitable initiative a formal structure, generally as a Trust, Society or Section 8 Company, under the applicable governing law.

Which NGO structure should I choose? +

It generally depends on your funding source, governance preferences and whether you plan to operate across states. Our experts can help you compare the options.

Can I register an NGO from my home address? +

A residential address may be used as a registered office where applicable, subject to the required documentation and legal requirements.

What documents are required? +

Typical requirements can include identity proof, address proof, photographs and registered-office documents. Exact requirements depend on the structure and applicant.

How long does NGO registration take? +

Processing time depends on the structure, documentation, government verification and whether additional clarification is required.

What happens after registration? +

Depending on the structure and activities, you may need to complete post-registration filings, tax-exemption applications, banking and ongoing compliance requirements.
