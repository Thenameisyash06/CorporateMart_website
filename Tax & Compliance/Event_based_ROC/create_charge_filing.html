OVERVIEW

What Is Charge Creation Filing?

Charge creation filing is the mandatory registration of a security interest — such as a mortgage, hypothecation or pledge — created on a company’s assets with the Registrar of Companies by filing Form CHG-1 under Section 77 of the Companies Act, 2013, within 30 days of charge creation.

Failure to register makes the charge void against the liquidator and creditors under Section 80, meaning the lender loses secured creditor status. Penalty under Section 86 includes a fine of ₹1 lakh to ₹10 lakh on the company and imprisonment up to 6 months for officers in default.

DOCUMENTS REQUIRED

Documents Required for Charge Creation Filing

1. Executed Charge Instrument

Loan agreement, mortgage deed, hypothecation or pledge agreement on appropriate stamp paper per state rates.

2. Certified Board Resolution

Resolution authorizing borrowing and charge creation, specifying the director authorized to sign the filing.

3. Company & Charge Holder Details

Company CIN, registered office details, charge holder PAN/CIN, name, address and amount secured.

4. Description of Charged Assets

Complete details of the property/assets charged, with valuation if applicable.

5. Digital Signature Certificate

Class 3 DSC of the authorized signatory, registered on the MCA portal.

6. Professional Certification

Mandatory Expert practising certificate for professional certification of Form CHG-1.

Pro tip: The charge instrument date must match the “Date of Creation” in CHG-1 exactly. Date mismatch is the most common rejection reason.

FILING PROCESS

Step-by-Step CHG-1 Filing Process

1. Execute the Charge Instrument

Complete the loan agreement, mortgage deed, hypothecation or pledge agreement on stamp paper. The instrument date starts the 30-day filing deadline.

2. Pass Board Resolution for Borrowing

Convene a board meeting and pass a resolution authorizing the borrowing and creation of charge. Specify loan amount, charge holder, assets and authorized director.

3. Gather Supporting Documents

Collect charge instrument, board resolution, charge holder details, property description and company CIN. Verify DSC validity.

4. Prepare Form CHG-1 on MCA Portal

Log in to MCA V3, open CHG-1, pre-fill with CIN. Enter charge creation date, charge holder details, amount secured, property description and type of charge.

5. Attach Documents and Verify

Upload executed instrument, board resolution and supplementary documents as PDFs (under 10 MB each). Cross-verify all details.

6. Obtain Professional Certification

Get the form certified by a practising Compliance Professional. This certification is mandatory and cannot be skipped.

7. Digitally Sign and Pay Government Fee

Apply DSC of authorized director and certifying professional. Pay government fee (₹200–₹5,000). SRN is generated upon payment.

8. Submit and Track Approval

Submit on MCA portal. ROC reviews, registers the charge and issues Charge ID with registration certificate. Processing typically 2–3 working days after submission.

CONDONATION

Condonation of Delay in Charge Filing

01

When Required

If CHG-1 is not filed within 30 days (or the extended window with additional fees), condonation under Section 87 is required.

02

Process

Application to Central Government / NCLT, additional government fees, 6x–10x penalty, and often Form CHG-9 for longer delays.

03

Cost & Time

Significantly higher cost and longer timeline (weeks to months) compared to on-time filing within 30 days.

04

Best Practice

Always file within 30 days of the charge instrument date to avoid condonation entirely.

CHG-1 VS CHG-4

CHG-1 vs CHG-4: Creation vs Satisfaction

Parameter CHG-1 CHG-4
Purpose Creation or modification of charge Satisfaction (full repayment) of charge
When Filed Within 30 days of charge creation Within 30 days of full satisfaction
Governing Section Section 77 Section 82
Key Outcome Charge ID + Registration Certificate Charge marked as satisfied on MCA
Professional Certification Mandatory Mandatory

CHG-1 registers a new or modified charge. CHG-4 is filed when the loan is fully repaid and the charge is satisfied. Both have a 30-day deadline and require professional certification.
