OVERVIEW

What is Authorized Share Capital?

Authorized share capital is the maximum amount of share capital a company can issue to shareholders, as defined under Section 2(8) of the Companies Act, 2013. It is specified in Clause V (Capital Clause) of the Memorandum of Association (MoA).

Increasing authorized capital is governed by Section 61 (power to alter share capital), Section 64 (notice to RoC via Form SH-7) and Section 13 (MoA amendment). The process requires an EGM resolution, stamp duty payment and MCA filing within 30 days of the resolution. Issuing shares beyond the authorized ceiling is void.

WHY INCREASE

Why Increase Authorized Share Capital?

01

Equity Funding Rounds

A startup with ₹1 lakh authorized capital raising ₹50 lakh in Series A must first increase the ceiling so investors can receive share allotment.

02

ESOP Pool Creation

ESOPs typically reserve 10–15% of share capital. Sufficient headroom is required before granting options.

03

Bonus Issue to Shareholders

Bonus shares under Section 63 are issued from reserves. Post-bonus issued capital must remain within authorized limits.

04

Debt-to-Equity Conversion

When lenders convert loans into equity, the company needs sufficient authorized capital to allot the conversion shares.

05

Regulatory Requirements

Certain licences mandate minimum capital (e.g. NBFCs ₹2 crore, insurance ₹100 crore). Authorized capital must cover these thresholds.

06

Strategic Investments & Mergers

During acquisitions or amalgamations, the acquiring company may need to issue shares to the target’s shareholders.

DOCUMENTS REQUIRED

Documents Required for Authorized Capital Increase

1. Board Resolution

Proposing the authorized capital increase with exact old and new capital figures, signed by attending directors.

2. EGM Notice

21 clear days’ notice to all shareholders under Section 101, with proposed resolution text and explanatory statement under Section 102.

3. EGM Minutes & Attendance

Detailed minutes recording the resolution, chairperson’s declaration and attendance sheet signed by all present.

4. Amended MoA

Updated Memorandum of Association reflecting the new authorized capital amount in Clause V.

5. Stamp Duty Challan

Proof of state-specific stamp duty payment via e-stamping portal on the increase amount.

6. Director’s DSC & Forms

Valid Class 3 DSC registered on MCA portal. Form MGT-14 if a special resolution was passed.

PROCESS

Step-by-Step Process to Increase Authorized Capital

1. Verify Articles of Association

Confirm whether the AoA authorizes capital increase by ordinary resolution under Section 61. If a special resolution is required, plan for 75% approval and MGT-14.

2. Convene Board Meeting

Hold a board meeting with 7-day notice under Section 173. Pass a board resolution proposing the exact increase amount and recommending an EGM.

3. Issue EGM Notice to Shareholders

Send EGM notice with minimum 21 clear days’ advance notice under Section 101, including the proposed resolution and explanatory statement under Section 102.

4. Hold EGM and Pass Resolution

Conduct the EGM with required quorum. Pass ordinary resolution (simple majority) or special resolution (75%) as applicable. Record detailed minutes and attendance.

5. Pay State-Specific Stamp Duty

Calculate and pay stamp duty on the increase amount (0.10%–0.15% by state, with caps). Pay via e-stamping portal and retain the challan for MCA filing.

6. File Form SH-7 on MCA Portal

File SH-7 within 30 days of the EGM resolution. Attach resolution, amended MoA and stamp duty challan. Pay government fee based on nominal capital slab. Sign with director’s DSC.

7. File Form MGT-14 (If Applicable)

If a special resolution was passed, file MGT-14 under Section 117 within 30 days. After RoC approval, download updated Master Data and proceed to issue shares under the new ceiling.

PENALTIES

Penalties for Late or Non-Compliance

01

Late SH-7 Filing

₹1,000 per day for the company (maximum ₹25 lakh) and per officer in default (maximum ₹5 lakh) under Section 64(2).

02

Issuing Beyond Authorized Capital

Issuing shares beyond authorized capital is void. Penalty of ₹10,000 plus ₹1,000 per day of continuing default under Section 450.

03

Non-Payment of Stamp Duty

Penalty of up to 10 times the unpaid stamp duty amount under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899.

04

Pending Annual Filings

Pending AOC-4 or MGT-7 may trigger SH-7 rejection. Clear all prior-year filings before starting the capital increase.
