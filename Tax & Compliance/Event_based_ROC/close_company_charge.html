OVERVIEW

What is Satisfaction of Charge?

Satisfaction of charge is the legal process of recording closure of a registered charge (loan security interest) with the Registrar of Companies by filing Form CHG-4 under Section 82 of the Companies Act, 2013, after full repayment of a secured loan.

When a company fully repays a secured loan, the charge registered via CHG-1 must be formally removed from MCA records. The company files CHG-4 within 30 days of the satisfaction date. The ROC sends a 14-day notice to the charge holder; if no objection is raised, the ROC issues Form CHG-5 (Certificate of Satisfaction).

The “satisfaction date” is when the lender confirms zero outstanding balance — not the date of the last EMI payment.

DOCUMENTS REQUIRED

Documents Required for Charge Satisfaction Filing

1. No Dues Certificate (NDC)

Original on lender’s letterhead with authorized signatory stamp, confirming zero outstanding balance.

2. Charge Release Letter

Referencing the Charge ID and loan account number, confirming release of the security interest.

3. Final Loan Closure Statement

Showing zero balance, dated within 30 days of the satisfaction date.

4. Certified Board Resolution

Authorizing CHG-4 filing, referencing charge ID, loan account and satisfaction date.

5. Copy of Original Charge Instrument

Loan agreement, mortgage deed or debenture trust deed that created the charge.

6. DSCs (Director + Bank)

Class 3 DSC of authorized director and of the charge holder’s authorized signatory, both registered on MCA.

FILING PROCESS

How to File Form CHG-4: Step-by-Step Process

1. Obtain No Dues Certificate from Lender

Request NDC and charge release letter from the bank after full repayment. Typically takes 3–5 working days. Send a formal request with loan account number and charge ID.

2. Pass Board Resolution

Convene a board meeting and pass a resolution authorizing a specific director to file Form CHG-4. Reference charge ID, loan account and satisfaction date.

3. Prepare Form CHG-4 on MCA Portal

Log in to MCA V3, fill Form CHG-4 with charge ID, satisfaction date and charge holder details. Upload NDC, board resolution and charge instrument copy.

4. Get Practising Professional Certification

A qualified practising professional must verify and certify Form CHG-4 with membership number and digital signature. Mandatory for filing.

5. Affix Digital Signatures

Authorized director and charge holder’s authorized signatory both digitally sign the form with Class 3 DSCs registered on MCA.

6. Pay Government Fee and Submit

Pay ₹200–₹600 based on authorized capital. Submit electronically and note the SRN for tracking.

7. ROC Sends 14-Day Notice to Charge Holder

ROC issues a 14-day notice under Section 82. Charge holder may raise objections. If none, ROC proceeds to register satisfaction.

8. Receive Certificate of Satisfaction (CHG-5)

After the notice period, ROC issues Form CHG-5. Charge status changes from “Open” to “Satisfied”. Update the company’s Register of Charges under Section 85.

CONDONATION

Condonation of Delay in Charge Satisfaction

01

When Required

If CHG-4 is not filed within 300 days of the satisfaction date, a separate condonation application (Form CHG-8) to the Regional Director is required.

02

Process

File CHG-8 with Central Government / Regional Director. After order, file INC-28 and then CHG-4 with the ROC.

03

Cost

Condonation typically costs ₹5,000 to ₹15,000+ in professional and government fees, plus significant time.

04

Best Practice

Always file within 30 days of the lender’s zero balance confirmation to avoid additional fees and condonation entirely.
