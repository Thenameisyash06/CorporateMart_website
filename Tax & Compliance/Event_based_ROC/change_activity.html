OVERVIEW

What Is Object Clause Amendment?

Object clause amendment is the legal process of altering the business activities listed in a company’s Memorandum of Association (MoA) under Section 13 of the Companies Act, 2013. It requires passing a special resolution with 75% shareholder majority and filing Form MGT-14 with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days.

Every company must declare its permitted business activities in the object clause of its MoA. Operating outside the declared object clause constitutes ultra vires activity, making contracts void and exposing directors to personal liability. Unlike the Companies Act, 1956, no prior central government approval is required under the 2013 Act.

DOCUMENTS REQUIRED

Documents Required for Object Clause Amendment

1. Existing Memorandum of Association

Current MoA with the existing object clause, obtained from company records or MCA portal.

2. Board Resolution

Resolution approving the proposed amendment and authorising the convening of an EGM.

3. EGM Notice (21 Clear Days)

Notice to all shareholders with date, time, venue, agenda, full text of proposed special resolution and explanatory statement under Section 102.

4. EGM Minutes & Attendance Sheet

Signed record of shareholders present and detailed minutes recording the special resolution and voting results.

5. Certified Special Resolution

Certified true copy of the special resolution confirming 75% majority approval, signed by a director or compliance professional.

6. Amended Object Clause + DSC

Draft of new/modified business activities with corresponding NIC codes, plus valid Class 3 DSC of the authorised signatory.

PROCESS

Step-by-Step Object Clause Amendment Process

1. Draft the Proposed Object Clause

Prepare the amended object clause with new business activities and corresponding NIC codes. Review existing MoA to identify objects to add, modify or remove.

2. Convene Board Meeting

Hold a board meeting to approve the proposed amendment and pass a board resolution authorising the EGM and the draft special resolution.

3. Issue EGM Notice

Send 21 clear days’ notice to all shareholders under Section 101, including the full text of the special resolution and explanatory statement under Section 102. Shorter notice possible with unanimous consent.

4. Hold EGM and Pass Special Resolution

Conduct the EGM with required quorum. Pass the special resolution with 75% shareholder majority by value of shares. Record attendance and minutes.

5. Prepare Form MGT-14

Fill Form MGT-14 on the MCA V3 portal. Attach certified copies of the special resolution, explanatory statement, EGM notice, minutes, attendance sheet, altered MoA and board resolution.

6. File Form MGT-14 with ROC

Submit Form MGT-14 within 30 days of the special resolution. Pay government fee (₹200–₹600 based on authorised capital). Late filing attracts ₹100/day under Section 403.

7. ROC Review and Approval

ROC reviews the filing and attachments (typically 3–7 working days). On approval, the company’s master data is updated on the MCA portal.

8. Receive Updated Certificate of Incorporation

Download the updated Certificate of Incorporation from the MCA portal confirming the amended object clause. Company CIN remains unchanged.

9. Update NIC Code & Post-Amendment Registrations

Update NIC code on MCA portal. Amend GST registration if new activities fall under different HSN/SAC codes. Update trade licence, bank records and obtain any sector-specific licences.

PENALTIES

Penalties for Late Filing & Ultra Vires Activities

01

Late MGT-14 Filing

Filing after 30 days of the special resolution attracts an additional fee of ₹100 per day under Section 403 of the Companies Act, 2013.

02

Ultra Vires Activities

Operating outside the declared object clause makes contracts void and exposes directors to personal liability. Amend before signing new contracts.

03

Incomplete Documentation

Uncertified or incomplete EGM documents lead to ROC rejection and 5–10 working days delay in re-filing.

04

Pending Annual Filings

Pending AOC-4 or MGT-7 may trigger MGT-14 rejection. Clear all prior-year filings before starting the amendment.

NIC CODE

NIC Code Updation After Amendment

01

What is NIC Code?

National Industrial Classification (NIC) codes categorise business activities for government filings, GST and statistical purposes. Each new activity in the object clause should be mapped to the correct NIC code.

02

Update on MCA Portal

After ROC approval of the object clause amendment, update the NIC code classification on the MCA portal to reflect the new business activities.

03

GST & Other Registrations

If new activities fall under different HSN/SAC codes, amend GST registration. Update trade licences and obtain any sector-specific licences required.

04

Why It Matters

Incorrect or missing NIC codes can cause issues with tenders, licences, FDI reporting and statistical compliance. Most providers miss this step — we include it.
