OVERVIEW

What is a Digital Signature Certificate?

A Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) is an electronic credential issued by a government-licensed Certifying Authority that authenticates the holder’s identity and enables secure digital signing of electronic documents. Under Section 3 of the Information Technology Act, 2000, digital signatures are legally recognised as equivalent to handwritten signatures.

DSC uses Public Key Infrastructure (PKI): a private key (stored on a USB crypto token) and a public key. Signing with the private key creates a unique signature that can be verified with the public key, ensuring authenticity and integrity. The Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) licenses CAs such as eMudhra, Sify, NSDL and others to issue DSCs after identity verification (typically video KYC).

PROCESS

How DSC is Obtained

1. Choose Type & Validity

Class 3 signing only or sign + encrypt; 1 or 2 years (2 years often more cost-effective).

2. Submit Documents

PAN, Aadhaar, photo and contact details to the Certifying Authority channel.

3. Video KYC

Complete identity verification via video call (webcam and microphone required).

4. Issuance & Token

Certificate generated and loaded on USB token; shipped or collected as arranged.

5. Install & Register

Install token drivers and register DSC on MCA, GST, IT or DGFT portals as needed.
