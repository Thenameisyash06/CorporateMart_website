OVERVIEW

What is a Factory License?

A Factory License is a mandatory permit issued under Section 6 of the Factories Act, 1948 by the Chief Inspector of Factories (State Factory Inspectorate). It authorises the occupier to operate a manufacturing premises and certifies compliance with health, safety, welfare and working condition standards under the Act and State Factory Rules.

Any premises where a manufacturing process is carried on with 10 or more workers using power, or 20 or more workers without power, is a “factory” under Section 2(m) and must obtain a valid license before operations. The license is premises- and process-specific and typically valid for one calendar year. Operating without it can attract fines up to ₹2,00,000, daily penalties and possible imprisonment under Sections 92–96.

Governing Law Factories Act, 1948 · Section 6
Authority Chief Inspector of Factories
Threshold 10+ with power / 20+ without
Validity Usually 1 calendar year
WHO NEEDS IT

When is a Factory License Required?

01

Manufacturing with Power

Premises where manufacturing is carried on with the aid of power and 10 or more workers are employed.

02

Manufacturing without Power

Premises where manufacturing is carried on without power and 20 or more workers are employed.

03

All Manufacturing Types

Food processing, textiles, chemicals, engineering, electronics and other manufacturing processes covered by the Act.

04

Hazardous Processes

Stricter requirements for processes listed in the First Schedule — Site Appraisal, emergency plans and additional clearances.

TYPES

Types of Applications

01

New Factory License

First-time license for a new factory — plan approval, Form 2, NOCs, inspection and full documentation.

02

Renewal

Annual renewal before expiry (typically by 31 December) with fee payment and updated compliance certificates.

03

Amendment

Changes in workers, machinery, process or layout — amendment application and revised plans where needed.

04

Plan Approval

Prior approval of building plans under Section 6 before construction or major modification of premises.

DOCUMENTS

What You Typically Need

1. Occupier Identity

PAN and Aadhaar of the occupier (and factory manager where required).

2. Building Plans

Site plan, floor plan, elevation and section drawings approved by a licensed architect/engineer.

3. Certificate of Stability

Form 25 (or equivalent) signed by a qualified structural engineer.

4. Premises Proof

Ownership deed, lease agreement or allotment letter for the factory premises.

5. NOCs

Fire NOC, Pollution Control Board consent (CTE/CTO) where applicable, municipal NOC.

6. Entity & Process

Certificate of Incorporation, list of machinery and manufacturing processes, electricity bill.

PROCESS

How Factory License Registration Works

1. Plan Approval

Submit building plans to the Chief Inspector of Factories and obtain approval before construction or start.

2. Document Preparation

Gather Form 2, stability certificate, NOCs, identity proofs, premises documents and process details.

3. Form 2 Filing

File application for registration and grant of license with the State Factory Inspectorate (online where available).

4. Inspection

Inspector of Factories visits the premises to verify plan compliance, safety, health and welfare provisions.

5. License (Form 3)

On approval, Factory License is granted in Form 3. Display at the factory and renew annually.

PENALTIES

Cost of Non-Compliance

01

Fine up to ₹2 Lakh

Under Section 92, operating without a valid license can attract a fine of up to ₹2,00,000 for the first offence.

02

Daily Penalty

After conviction, continuing offence may attract an additional penalty of ₹1,000 per day.

03

Imprisonment

Possible imprisonment of up to 2 years under the Act for serious or continued defaults.

04

Closure Orders

Authorities may order closure of the factory until a valid license is obtained and compliance is restored.
