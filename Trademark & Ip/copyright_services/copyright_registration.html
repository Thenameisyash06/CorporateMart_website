OVERVIEW

What is Copyright Registration?

Copyright registration is the legal process of recording ownership of an original creative work with the Copyright Office under Section 45 of the Copyright Act, 1957. It provides prima facie evidence of ownership and strengthens civil and criminal enforcement against infringement.

Under Section 13, copyright protection arises automatically when an original work is created in tangible form. Registration is not mandatory, but it creates a public record that Indian courts treat as prima facie proof of ownership. Applications are filed using Form XIV through the e-filing portal at copyright.gov.in. India’s membership in the Berne Convention extends protection to 181 countries without separate filings in each country.

KEY BENEFITS

Why Register Your Copyright?

01

Prima Facie Evidence of Ownership

Registration under Section 45 creates prima facie evidence in Indian courts. Without it, you must prove ownership through drafts, timestamps and witnesses.

02

Licensing & Monetisation

A registered copyright can be licensed exclusively or non-exclusively under Section 30. Earn royalties while retaining ownership.

03

Protection in 181 Countries

Berne Convention membership means your Indian registration supports protection in 181 member countries without separate local filings.

04

Civil & Criminal Remedies

Stronger standing for injunctions, damages (Section 55) and criminal action under Section 63 against infringers.

05

Deterrent Effect

A public registration deters potential infringers who can see that ownership is formally recorded with the Copyright Office.

06

Long Term of Protection

Author’s lifetime + 60 years for most works; 60 years from publication for films and sound recordings — one of the longest IP terms.

PROCESS

8-Step Copyright Registration Process

1. Identify Work Type

Classify the work under Section 13 (literary, dramatic, musical, artistic, cinematograph film or sound recording). Confirm originality and tangible form.

2. Title / Prior Search (Optional)

Search the Copyright Office records if relevant to avoid conflict or duplicate claims. Not mandatory but useful for published works.

3. Prepare Form XIV

Complete Form XIV under Rule 70 with title, nature of work, author and applicant details, year of creation/publication and Statement of Particulars.

4. E-File & Pay Fee

File online on copyright.gov.in, upload copies of the work in the prescribed format and pay the government fee (₹500–₹5,000 depending on work type).

5. Diary Number & 30-Day Wait

Receive a diary number. A mandatory 30-day objection period runs. Interested parties may file objections during this window.

6. Examination

The Copyright Office examines the application, documents and work. Queries or discrepancies may be raised for response.

7. Objection / Discrepancy Response

If an objection or discrepancy is raised, file a response within the prescribed time with supporting documents or clarifications.

8. Registration Certificate

On acceptance, the work is entered in the Register of Copyrights and a registration certificate is issued. Typical timeline: 2–3 months if no objections.

DOCUMENTS REQUIRED

What you need for Copyright Registration

1. Copies of the Work

Literary: PDF; Artistic: JPEG/PDF; Sound: MP3; Software: first 10 + last 10 pages of source code; Film: digital copy or link. Clear and complete.

2. Identity Proof

PAN, Aadhaar or Passport of the applicant or authorised signatory.

3. Address Proof

Utility bill, bank statement or Aadhaar matching the application address.

4. Statement of Particulars

Description of the work, purpose, creation details and first publication (if any) as required in Form XIV.

5. Power of Attorney (if via agent)

Notarised Power of Attorney / Vakalatnama when filing through an advocate or agent.

6. NOC / Other (if applicable)

NOC from publisher, co-authors or employer if the work is published or created under employment, as relevant.
