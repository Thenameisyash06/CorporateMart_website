OVERVIEW

What is Patent Registration in India?

Patent registration is the legal process of filing an application with the Indian Patent Office under the Patents Act, 1970 to secure exclusive commercial rights over an invention for 20 years, provided it meets novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability criteria.

Administered by the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM) under DPIIT, the system follows a first-to-file principle. The Indian Patent Office operates from Delhi (head office), Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, with electronic filing through ipindia.gov.in. A granted patent gives the right to exclude others from making, using, selling or importing the invention without permission. Unauthorized use can lead to civil remedies including injunctions and damages.

PATENTABILITY

Essential Requirements Under the Patents Act

01

Novelty (Section 2(1)(l))

The invention must be new and not disclosed to the public anywhere in the world before the filing date. Prior publication, use or disclosure destroys novelty. India has no grace period.

02

Inventive Step (Section 2(1)(ja))

The invention must involve a technical advance or economic significance that is not obvious to a person skilled in the art.

03

Industrial Applicability (Section 2(1)(ac))

The invention must be capable of being made or used in some kind of industry, including agriculture.

04

Not Excluded (Sections 3 & 4)

The invention must not fall under non-patentable subject matter listed in Sections 3 and 4 of the Patents Act, 1970 (e.g. abstract ideas, certain methods, atomic energy).

Critical: Publicly disclosing your invention before filing destroys novelty. File at least a provisional specification before publishing papers, presenting at conferences or launching products without an NDA.
TYPES

Types of Patents / Inventions

01

Product Patents

Protect the product itself regardless of how it is made — e.g. pharmaceutical drugs, chemical compounds, mechanical devices.

02

Process Patents

Protect methods of manufacturing or producing something — e.g. synthesis routes, manufacturing processes.

03

Utility / Machine / Composition

New and useful processes, machines, manufactures or compositions of matter that meet the three patentability criteria.

04

Provisional vs Complete

Provisional specification secures a priority date quickly; complete specification (with claims) must be filed within 12 months to proceed to examination and grant.

PROCESS

Step-by-Step Patent Registration Process

1. Prior Art Search

Search InPASS, Google Patents and other databases to assess novelty and inventive step. A strong search reduces later FER objections and wasted fees.

2. Draft Specification

Prepare provisional or complete specification under Section 10 — description, claims, abstract and drawings. Claims define the breadth of protection.

3. File with IPO (Forms 1, 2, 3, 5, 28)

Submit on ipindia.gov.in. Government fee depends on applicant category (₹1,600 for individual/startup e-filing for provisional/complete up to limits). Secure priority date.

4. Publication

Application is published after 18 months (or earlier on request via Form 9). Public can inspect the specification.

5. Request for Examination (Form 18 / 18A)

Must be filed within 48 months of priority/filing date. Without RFE the application is deemed withdrawn. Expedited examination (Form 18A) available for startups and eligible applicants.

6. Respond to FER

First Examination Report raises objections (prior art, Section 3/4, clarity). Reply within the prescribed period (typically 6 months) with amendments and arguments.

7. Grant & Certificate

On acceptance, the patent is granted and a certificate is issued. Term is 20 years from the filing date. Annual renewal fees apply from the 3rd year.

DOCUMENTS REQUIRED

What you need for Patent Filing

1. Form 1 — Application

Main application with applicant and inventor details, title of invention and address for service.

2. Form 2 — Specification

Provisional or complete specification with description, claims, abstract and drawings as applicable.

3. Form 3 — Section 8 Statement

Statement regarding corresponding foreign applications filed or to be filed.

4. Form 5 — Inventorship

Declaration of true and first inventor(s).

5. Form 28 — Entity Declaration

Declaration of applicant category (natural person, startup, small entity, other) for correct fee.

6. Form 26 — Power of Attorney

Authorisation for the patent agent to file and prosecute when filing through an agent.
